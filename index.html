SeedSigner

AI Analysis

A series of AI-assisted analyses of SeedSigner's critical subsystems. Each one explains how a subsystem actually works, establishes whether it was ever weak in any earlier release, and includes the instructions to check the work independently.

These are not independent audits and not official SeedSigner project publications. They are individual analyses produced by SeedSigner’s lead developer using AI tooling, published because “trust us” is a poor answer to questions about how a Bitcoin wallet generates keys. Every factual claim is pinned to a source line or a measurement, and every document carries a record of what it could not verify.

If an analysis is corrected after publication, the correction is recorded in the document itself: what changed and why, not a silent edit. A document that hides its own revision history is not a transparency document.

Reading these

Each analysis follows the same shape, documented in CONVENTIONS.md: what the implementation does, how it was verified, results, every release checked, degenerate cases, an adjudication record, what was not verified, and how to reproduce it.

Once an analysis grows past a few thousand words it splits in two and the two halves are written for different readers. The analysis is for a person deciding how to use the device and carries everything needed to use it safely. The data and methodology is the working behind it: canonical figures, the release history, the adjudication record and the reproduction commands. That half takes an AI-first approach, organized so claims can be checked one by one, and increasingly an AI reviewer is who actually consumes it. Pointing a model at it is the fastest way to get a claim checked, though it is written to stay readable for anyone who wants to work through it directly.

The adjudication record is the section to read before reporting a problem. It lists every concern that was raised and consciously dispositioned, with the reasoning and, more usefully, an explicit note on where each disposition is weakest. Those weak points are where a reviewer should start.

Contributing a correction

Corrections and challenges are welcome, particularly on the measurements. Every analysis includes reproduction instructions and ships the raw data it was derived from, so a disagreement can be settled with numbers rather than opinion. Open an issue.

If you are directing an AI to review this project, read the deployment-reality notes in each analysis first. Reviewers have repeatedly produced confident findings that do not apply to air-gapped hardware with no wireless and no persistent filesystem.
